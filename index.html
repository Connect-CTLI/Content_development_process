CTLI Design Hub

Choose the programme team and add the course draft. The Hub reviews it against UDL and the Moodle course flow for you to approve, then snippets the draft into resources, fills in the Resource Creation, Question Bank, Snippets and Design Blueprint tabs, and gives you the sheet back to download. Once the developers have chosen their elements, it makes each developer a pack of their content and a timesheet, and turns their completed timesheets into claim lines for the PDD Support Claim. Files are read in your browser and never uploaded.

2 Add the original course draft

The draft is checked against Universal Design for Learning (UDL) and the CTLI Moodle course flow. It must use the Course Draft template: Heading 1 for each module (starting "Module"), Heading 2 for each topic, and bold lines for activities such as "Assignment:", "Reflective post:" or "Knowledge check:".

3 Learning design review

Each suggestion shows the original on the left and what to do on the right. Most come with ready-made text written from the draft: pick one, write your own version, keep it as a comment only, or leave it out. The draft is then made with your choices: download the reviewed draft to see them in Word as tracked changes (highlighted) and comments, or download it with the changes already made. Then add the approved draft in step 4.

Add a course draft in step 2.

What the review checks
  • Engagement (why we learn): a welcome and relevance to participants' schools, knowledge checks with feedback, reflection.
  • Representation (what we learn): more than one way to take in key content, chunked topics, short sentences and paragraphs, acronyms written out, key takeaways.
  • Action & Expression (how we show learning): observable objectives, time to complete, a choice of format for assignments, success criteria.
  • Accessibility: descriptive link text, alt text on images, tables introduced and with header rows.
  • Moodle flow: the six course page tabs, then each module as overview, content, activity, knowledge check, reflection and key takeaways, and a course feedback survey at the end.

8 Developer packs

9 Timesheets and claims

Add the developers' completed timesheets (T08, or the older content developer timesheet). The Hub checks them, adds up the time per support category, logs the time against each element in the Resource Creation Sheet, and sends each developer's month to their Workplan and Time Schedule, which the PDD Support Claim reads. Payment is still worked out in the claim sheet: the Hub carries no rates and no personal details.
